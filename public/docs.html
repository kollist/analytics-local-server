1. Where the data comes from #

The mobile apps send a stream of events (screen views, taps, purchases, errors…) to this server. Each event is stored as one row with a timestamp, the app, the platform, an anonymous device id, a session id, an event type, and a JSON properties blob.

Revenue and order metrics are counted entirely from the purchase event. Its properties.value is the order total. There is an older order_placed / place_order_tapped event that only fires for a minority of orders — it is deliberately not used in any metric, to avoid undercounting.

Excluded apps

Apps that have been pulled from the App Store, plus internal template / sandbox builds (vcc, vccsandbox), are kept out of the “All apps” aggregate and the leaderboard so they don’t drag the averages down. The list is synced from App Store Connect. Selecting a single app from the filter always shows that app’s real numbers, excluded or not.

2. Filters & time windows #

The three dropdowns in the header apply to the whole page:

  • App — one app, or all apps (excluded apps removed).
  • Platform — iOS / Android / all.
  • Time window — 7, 30, 90, or 365 days, or All time.

A window of N days means events with a timestamp in the last N×24 hours, up to now. All time removes the lower bound entirely — every event ever received.

The App Leaderboard ignores the App filter (it always compares every app) but does respect the Platform filter and the time window.

3. Data freshness #

The dashboard reads from a cache in front of the database, so numbers are near-real-time but not to-the-second:

WindowRefresh cadenceWhy
7 / 30 / 90 dayscontinuous, ≤ ~30 s oldCheap to compute; kept permanently warm.
365 days / All timeevery ~5 minutesThese scan the entire events table on every sub-query. Recomputing them on every page poll would saturate the server, and totals at that scale barely move minute to minute.

When a set of numbers is more than ~90 seconds old, the grey subtitle under the header shows “updated N m ago”. That is expected on the 365-day and All-time views — it is not an error.

4. KPI strip #

The seven cards at the top of the dashboard. All are for the selected window, app and platform.

CardDefinition
Orders Number of purchase events in the window. A session that checks out twice counts as 2 orders.
Orders / day The big number is always the all-time average: total purchases ever ÷ the number of days between the first and last purchase (respecting the app / platform filter). It does not change when you switch windows — only the grey subtitle does, which adds the windowed rate (orders in window ÷ days in window). On the All-time view the subtitle just reads “avg over N days”.
GMV Gross Merchandise Value — the sum of properties.value across all purchase events in the window. Gross: before refunds, fees or cancellations.
Conversion Session-based. sessions with a purchase ÷ all sessions × 100 Counted on distinct session_id. Not device-based and not order-based.
Avg Order Value Order-based. GMV ÷ Orders (using the purchase-event count, so consistent with the Orders card).
Crash-free sessions (1 − sessions with a crash ÷ all sessions) × 100 A “crash” is an app_error event whose properties.type is "crash". Non-crash errors (network, login) do not affect this number — see the Errors page for those.
New users Distinct devices (anonymous_id) active in the window that have no event of any kind before the window started — i.e. first seen during this window. On the All-time view this equals total users.
Sessions vs users vs devices. “Sessions” are app-open sessions (session_id). “Users” / “devices” are anonymous_id — one per install, not per person. Someone who reinstalls or uses two phones is two users. There is no login-based identity in these metrics.

5. The “vs previous period” deltas #

The small coloured line under most KPIs (▲ 12% / ▼ 4pp) compares the current window to the equal-length window immediately before it. Last 30 days is compared to the 30 days before that.

  • % — relative change ((now − before) ÷ before). Used for counts and money.
  • pp — percentage points. Used for Conversion and Crash-free, because a move from 2% → 3% is “+1pp”, not “+50%”.
  • new — there was no activity in the previous period to compare against.
  • Colour is green when the change is in the good direction for that metric, red otherwise.
Deltas are hidden entirely on the All-time view — there is no “period before all time”.

6. Trend charts #

The three small charts: Orders/day, Conversion/day, GMV/day. The x-axis bucket size adapts to the window:

SpanBucket
up to ~13 weeksone bar per day
up to ~2 yearsone bar per week (Sunday-anchored)
longerone bar per month

The large number in each chart’s corner is a summary over the visible buckets:

  • GMV chart — total revenue across the buckets. Matches the GMV KPI.
  • Orders chart — total sessions that purchased across the buckets. This can be slightly lower than the Orders KPI, which counts purchase events (one session, two purchases = 2 orders but 1 on the chart).
  • Conversion chart — the unweighted average of each bucket’s conversion rate. Over a long window with quiet early buckets this can differ noticeably from the Conversion KPI (which is one ratio over the whole window). When they disagree, trust the KPI.
The first and last buckets are usually partial (the window starts mid-week, today is mid-month), so the end bars often look like a dip. That is the bucket being incomplete, not a traffic drop.

7. App Leaderboard #

Per-app breakdown for the selected window and platform. Always covers every app (the App filter does not apply here); removed / sandbox apps are excluded.

Columns

GMVSum of purchase value for that app.
Conv %Purchasing sessions ÷ sessions, for that app.
AOVGMV ÷ orders, for that app.
UsersDistinct devices for that app.

Tabs

  • Top performers — every app, sortable by any column (default: GMV, high to low).
  • Best converting — apps with at least 30 sessions in the window, ranked by conversion rate, highest first. The 30-session floor keeps a 1-session-1-order app off the top.
  • Underperformers — same 30-session floor, ranked by conversion rate, lowest first.

Click a row to load that app into the page filter; click a column header (Top performers tab) to re-sort.

8. Ordering Funnel #

Five steps on the path to an order. Each bar is the number of distinct sessions in which that event occurred during the window:

StepEvent
Browse menuview_item_list
View itemview_item
Add to cartadd_to_cart
Begin checkoutbegin_checkout
Purchasepurchase

The two right-hand columns

Of top This step ÷ the first step (Browse menu). The cumulative share of everyone who entered the funnel that reached this step.
Step conversion This step ÷ the previous step, shown as “X% kept” with the number of sessions lost in red, e.g. (−500). It isolates which single step is leaking. The first row shows “entry” instead — it is the top of the funnel, so there is no earlier step to convert from.

Example — Browse 1,000 → View item 800 → Add to cart 300:

  • View item: Of top 80%, Step conversion “80% kept (−200)”
  • Add to cart: Of top 30%, Step conversion “38% kept (−500)”
This is not a strict ordered path. Each step just asks “did this session ever fire this event in the window”. A session that adds to cart without a recorded view_item still counts in Add to cart. Consequences: “Step conversion” can read above 100%, and multiplying the step rates together will not exactly equal “Of top”. Read it as a diagnostic of where drop-off concentrates, not as an exact cohort trace.

place_order_tapped is left out of the funnel on purpose: only ~15% of purchases emit it, which would make the funnel appear to collapse to near-zero and then recover.

9. Checkout Health #

All figures are for the selected window. “Started checkouts” = distinct sessions with a begin_checkout event.

Checkout abandonment1 − (sessions that purchased ÷ sessions that started checkout). The subtitle shows the raw “X of Y completed”.
Abandoned cart valueSum of properties.cart_value on checkout_abandoned events; the count is the number of those events.
Failed ordersCount of order_place_failed events — payment declined, server error, etc. (Detail on the Errors page.)
Checkouts with a couponcoupon_applied events ÷ started checkouts (capped at 100%). “$X discounted” is the sum of properties.discount.
Checkouts with a tiptip_selected events ÷ started checkouts. “avg tip” is the mean properties.amount.
Order-type selectionsCount of order_type_selected events. The split bar below is delivery vs pickup, from properties.is_delivery.
Payment methodsCount of payment_method_selected events grouped by properties.method.
Coupon / tip rates are counted as events per started checkout, so a session that applies a coupon twice is counted twice (hence the 100% cap). Treat them as directional.

10. Top Items #

The 15 products with the most add_to_cart events in the window, by properties.product_name. Columns: total add-to-carts, the number of distinct sessions that added it, and the average properties.price seen. This is an interest signal (adds), not a sales-by-item report — it does not track whether the cart converted.

11. Detail section #

The collapsed “Detail” block at the bottom:

  • Events by type — raw count of every event type in the window, grouped by category. Error events are excluded here (they have their own page).
  • Avg time per screen — from screen_exited’s duration_ms, in seconds. Only durations between 0 and 10 minutes are counted; anything longer is treated as the user leaving the phone on the screen and is dropped, so the averages aren’t skewed by outliers.
  • Notes — auto-generated one-liners: the biggest funnel drop, abandoned-cart total, longest screen, and a pointer to the Errors page.

12. Errors page #

A separate view (Errors) for failures. Three types:

  • App errors (app_error) — crashes and network errors.
  • Order failures (order_place_failed).
  • Login failures (login_failed).

The pills at the top are exact totals for the window. The grouped table below clusters errors by a generated signature (type + message) — but it is built from the most recent 5,000 matching error events only. For a high-volume window the per-group counts are therefore a recent sample, not the full-window total. First-seen / last-seen / affected-apps on each group are likewise within that sample.

13. FAQ — likely questions #

Why doesn’t “Orders” match the count of order_placed events?
It shouldn’t. Orders are counted from purchase. order_placed is a legacy event that fires for only a fraction of orders and is used nowhere.
Why does the All-time view say “updated 4m ago”?
By design. The 365-day and All-time views are expensive to compute, so they refresh on a ~5-minute cycle instead of every poll. The label is there so a lagging refresh can’t be mistaken for live data.
Why is the GMV for “Last 365 days” different from “All time”?
All time includes every order ever; 365 days only includes the last year. If almost all revenue is recent they’ll be close, but All time is always ≥ 365 days. (If you ever see the reverse, one of the views is mid-refresh — reload in a minute.)
The funnel says 80% × 50% × 60%… but “Of top” doesn’t match that product. Bug?
No — see section 8. The steps are counted independently (event present in the session, yes/no), not as a strict A→B→C path, so the per-step rates don’t chain exactly.
Why is the conversion number on the chart different from the Conversion KPI?
The chart corner shows the average of each day/week/month’s conversion rate; the KPI is one ratio over the whole window. Quiet early buckets pull the chart average around. Trust the KPI.
Why is app X missing from the leaderboard?
Either it has no events in the window, or it’s on the excluded list (pulled from the App Store, or an internal vcc/vccsandbox build). Pick it in the App filter to see its real numbers.
Do these numbers count real people?
They count devices (anonymous_id, one per install) and sessions, not authenticated users. Reinstalls and multi-device usage inflate user counts.
Is GMV net of refunds?
No. GMV is gross — the sum of purchase values at the moment of purchase. Refunds, cancellations and fees are not subtracted.
What timezone are the day buckets in?
UTC.
